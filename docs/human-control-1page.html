How humans stay in control

Cressey decides nothing a human cannot change. A model never releases a hold. Thresholds are in backend/data/policy.yaml and can be changed live with PUT /api/policy/responses.

Modes: alert lets the call through and opens an incident; review holds it for a human; stop refuses it at once. After 3 rejected calls in a turn, Cressey tells the agent to stop and report. Holds never self-release: a person approves, rejects, or lets it expire.

Traceability of AI decisions

  • Append-only event log. Each event stores the previous event's hash and its own SHA-256. verify() checks sequence, links and hashes, and can be anchored outside the log. A tampered or reordered event fails. The demo log verified: 15 records, chain intact.
  • Every hold states why: the reason, the step number, the confirmed triangle legs, and the score.
  • Sourced guides: each step cites a retrieved passage (ANSSI, MITRE ATLAS, OWASP, internal runbooks). Citation validity was 100%. A step citing a passage outside the retrieved list is dropped in code.
  • Reasoning is narration. No provider exposes raw chain of thought, so the dashboard shows what the agent said, not why its model chose it.

Open gaps

  • Holds tire the approver. A SOC analyst who cannot judge a developer's file edit gets every write held (open). Planned fix: hold only on alarms, and report holds per 100 normal runs. Not done.
  • Fail-open on Claude Code before 2.1.295 (above).
  • Judge can be swayed. Adversarial text raised the score in our test but never lowered it. Tested with Jev only.
  • Report check misses by design: 5 of 10 lies at 0.8.
